# Writing logic: Table and figure captions

Each item gives what to write and what to check.

## Table captions

- What to write: The first sentence says what is compared and on which model or dataset. From the second sentence on, give what is needed to read the table and does not fit in the header, such as raw counts, how to read a row or a parenthesis, what a column measures, symbols, abbreviations, the scope of the experiment and the data source.
- Check: Read the caption, the header, the table notes and the main text together; information needed to read the table only has to appear in one of them. The meaning of bold and other marks is stated in one of them.

## Figure captions

- What to write: Every figure caption has three parts, in order: the title, the meaning of the panels, the conclusion.
  - Title: one sentence saying what the figure is.
  - Meaning of the panels: what each column, row or panel is, and the reading conventions.
  - Conclusion: one or two sentences on what the figure shows; write a second sentence only when there really is a second comparison.
- Check:
  - The conclusion relies only on conclusions already written in the outline, and does not repeat the walkthrough of the example in the main text.
  - An example figure in the introduction states the problem its comparison shows, without praising the method; a comparison figure in the experiments states what each side of the comparison achieves.
  - The overview figure of the method is described along the flow of the method, not the top-to-bottom layout of the figure; explain only the symbols needed to read it.

## Decide before writing

- Whether bold is explained in the caption, in a table note, or not at all
- Whether metrics and units go in the caption or in the header
- Whether table captions carry a conclusion
- Whether a group of tables uses parallel or varied sentence patterns
- Whether figure captions mention colors
