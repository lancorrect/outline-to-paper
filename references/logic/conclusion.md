# Writing logic: Conclusion

Each item gives what to write and what to check.

The usual line of the conclusion is: arrive at the paper, the key designs, what the paper achieved, and optionally an insight or value. The conclusion is one paragraph and reports no numbers; these two points are fixed and not asked.

## Opening

- What to write: Get to the paper quickly: the proposed method and its positioning. One sentence naming the problem solved may come first.
- Check: Do not restart from the background of the field, prior work or examples of the challenges. The framework the paper builds on is not presented as its own.

## Key designs

- What to write: For each component, keep the key action and its purpose, so that readers can recognize the contribution.
- Check: Do not restate formulas, scoring, thresholds or execution order; do not reduce it to words such as "flexible" or "effective" that show no difference. The components appear in the same order as in the introduction.

## What the paper achieved

- What to write: One sentence that sums up the overall comparison; if needed, one more that sums up what the component analyses found.
- Check: Do not replay the RQs one by one. No numbers. The strength matches the experiments section.

## Insight or value (optional)

- What to write: An insight the reader should remember, or the value of the work.
- Check: The insight returns to a design or finding the paper has already discussed; no new claim at the end. If nothing fits, stop at the results; do not invent future work or limitations to round off the ending.

## Decide before writing

- Whether the last sentence stops at the results, states the value, or points to future directions
- Whether to state limitations
